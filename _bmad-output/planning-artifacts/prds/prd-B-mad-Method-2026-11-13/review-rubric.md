# PRD Quality Review — Rebat

## Overall verdict
This PRD is unusually honest about what it doesn't know, which is its real strength: constraints, open questions, and scope exclusions are sourced and specific rather than smoothed over, and the deliberate hand-off of FR-level detail to the three per-surface user-story docs is executed coherently (every scope-table row carries a citation, with one exception). Where it's weaker is in the connective tissue a downstream reader needs — no Glossary, inconsistent ID conventions across the three source docs, and a Success Metrics section that (for defensible reasons) leaves the stated thesis with nothing to validate it, even provisionally. Nothing here is broken; the risks are in drift and verification once this PRD is used as the index into docs this review could not independently check.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Trade-offs are named with what's given up, not just what's chosen: §5 states compliance (WASL) is mocked and not yet resolved, that the Absher gating behavior is unconfirmed and safety-relevant, and that the ride-alone minimum-rider rule has changed three times with current status unconfirmed. §9's eleven open questions are genuinely open — each names the conflicting sources rather than asserting an answer and hiding the conflict (e.g. #5 and #6 on Plane's unreliable "Done" state). Objective #5 ("without blocking launch indefinitely on them") and Objective #7 name real tensions rather than "balancing" them away. This is the opposite of the rubric's red flag — nothing here reads as uniformly safe.

### Findings
- **low** Objective #7 reads as aspirational rather than decided (§3) — "a single console... without needing engineering involvement for routine interventions" is a goal statement with no stated trade-off (what operations gives up to get this, or what's currently not true). *Fix:* either cut it to the concrete capability claim or add what's still engineering-dependent today.

## Substance over theater — strong
No persona theater: three personas (Student, Driver, Admin), each with a Role/Goal that maps directly to scope rows in §6 rather than padding. No Vision theater: the Overview (§1) is specific to this product's actual state (pilot city, build status, platform gaps), not a swappable paragraph. No NFR theater: the PRD avoids a generic NFR section entirely rather than filling one with "must be scalable/secure" boilerplate — the Saudi-market requirements in §5 are concrete (RTL, SAR, Hijri/Gregorian, phone-only identity) rather than adjectives. No innovation/differentiation section exists to be theater.

### Findings
(none — this dimension has no findings worth adding)

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is explicit and holds together: ad hoc, unpredictable student transport → managed subscription + automated matching + continuity + live tracking + named safety safeguards (§2, §3). Scope (§6) and Out of Scope (§7) both trace back to that thesis rather than reading as an unordered capability list — e.g., instant/on-demand trips are explicitly excluded because they contradict the subscription/continuity thesis, not because they're merely deferred. Where it weakens: §8 Success Metrics deliberately carries no targets, which is the right call given the sourcing problem the PRD describes, but it means the PRD's thesis has literally nothing — not even a placeholder direction — to be validated against. The production snapshot (~86 drivers, ~1,248 subscriptions, ~312 trips/day) is given as a baseline, not linked to what "working" would look like (e.g., continuity/retention, which is the thesis's actual bet, isn't mentioned as a metric category even provisionally).

### Findings
- **medium** Success Metrics has no thesis-linked placeholder categories (§8) — omitting invented targets is correct, but omitting the *shape* of what should be measured (continuity rate, on-time rate, driver retention) leaves the PRD's central bet untestable even in principle. *Fix:* add a short "metric categories to define" list tied to the thesis (continuity, safety-incident rate, settlement reliability) without inventing numbers, so the gap is scoped rather than blank.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Given the deliberate shape (FR-level acceptance criteria and Built/Unconfirmed/In design status live in the three user-story docs, cited by ID), this dimension should be judged on whether the hand-off holds, and mostly it does: every row in the §6 scope table carries at least one citation into Student/Driver/Admin. The one break is real: the M11 Admin cell for "SOS alerts screen" has no ID at all — it's cited as "live in production, not yet in the design file, see §9" (§6, §9 item 9). That is the single safety-critical capability in the table where a downstream reader has nothing to extract from; everywhere else the deferral mechanism works as designed. Separately, §3 Objectives make claims ("transparent," "predictable weekly settlement," "a single console") that are reasonable at the objective level but have no pointer to where their done-ness criteria live, unlike §6's scope rows — a minor asymmetry in how rigorously the PRD treats its own sections.

### Findings
- **medium** Admin "SOS alerts" capability has no resolvable ID (§6 M11, cross-referenced §9 item 9) — the one place in the scope table where the citation mechanism breaks down is also the one involving active safety monitoring. *Fix:* get an ID assigned for this screen (in Plane or the Admin doc) even if its status is "undocumented," rather than leaving the table cell as a forward reference to an open question.
- **low** §3 Objectives aren't cross-referenced to where their acceptance criteria live (unlike §6 Scope rows) — a reader hitting "transparent, explained commission structure" in §3 has no pointer to the Driver doc section that defines it. *Fix:* either drop objective-level claims to pure direction-setting language, or add the same doc-citation pattern used in §6.

## Scope honesty — strong
This is the PRD's best dimension. Out of Scope (§7) ties every exclusion to a source rather than asserting it ("none of these appear in any source for this product" for parent/guardian accounts; "unconfirmed as live, per the driver doc" for automated payout). §9's eleven open questions are substantive, not rhetorical — several (Absher gating, the ride-alone rule's current state, the SOS-alerts/design-file gap) are safety- or trust-relevant and stated as genuinely unresolved rather than softened. The density (11 open items) is high for a document describing a live product, but each is sourced and specific rather than padding, which is the distinction the rubric asks for.

### Findings
- **medium** Most open questions have no named owner or resolution deadline (§9 items 1–3, 7–10) — only item 11 names an owner ("Samah"). For #1 (Absher gating a new driver's first trip), which is explicitly flagged as the highest-priority safety item, the absence of an owner or date is a bigger gap than for the others. *Fix:* add an owner and target date to at least the safety- and compliance-critical items (1, 2, 3, 7, 9).
- **low** Open question #6 (Plane's "Done" state being unreliable) is process/tooling commentary about how to read Plane, not an unresolved product decision — it dilutes the list's focus from decisions a PM needs to make. *Fix:* move it to a short methodology note (e.g., in the sources line at the top) rather than the Open Questions list.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The hand-off to the three user-story docs is the PRD's organizing idea, and the mechanics mostly hold: citations are present for nearly every scope-table row, and the "sources in order of authority" framing at the top gives a reader a clear resolution order when docs disagree. Two things would cause friction for a story-writer or architect pulling from this PRD in isolation. First, there is no Glossary: terms used once in the scope table without definition elsewhere — "window coverage," "continuity" (as a matching criterion), "cover requests," "subscription route," "notice-and-consent" — are domain-specific enough that drift is likely once other documents start using them. Second, the citation convention is inconsistent across the three source docs as cited here: Student and Admin use ID prefixes (ST-xx, OP-xx) while Driver is cited by section number (§x.x) — not wrong, since that may reflect how each source doc is actually structured, but the PRD doesn't flag the asymmetry, so a reader could assume all three have the same ID discipline.

### Findings
- **medium** No Glossary section — several domain terms (window coverage, continuity, cover requests, subscription route, notice-and-consent) appear once in §3/§6 without definition. *Fix:* add a short glossary even if terse; it's the cheapest fix with the highest downstream payoff given three separate docs will reuse these terms.
- **low** Citation convention differs by source doc (Student/Admin: ID prefixes; Driver: section numbers) with no note explaining why (§6). *Fix:* one sentence noting the Driver doc uses section references rather than discrete IDs, so downstream readers don't infer a missing ID scheme.

## Shape fit — strong
The capability-spec shape — no FR-ID list, no named-protagonist UJs, no numeric NFR section in the PRD body — is a deliberate and well-justified choice given the three already-written, code-verified user-story docs carry that detail with Built/Unconfirmed/In design status. This also correctly reads as a brownfield + regulatory-constraint shape: the PRD explicitly distinguishes the unrelated earlier draft from the actual shipped product (header note, §7), and treats WASL/ELM and Absher not as NFR boilerplate but as named, dated, unresolved launch blockers (§5, §9) — which is exactly what constraint traceability should look like for a regulated service.

### Findings
- **low** The product has at least one safety-critical, consumer-facing flow (ride-alone notice-and-consent) that a reader of this PRD alone cannot picture end-to-end, since no UJ exists even as an anchor. *Fix:* consider one short illustrative journey for the highest-stakes flow only (not a full UJ set) — enough to let a reader without access to the Student doc understand what's at stake in open question #2.

## Mechanical notes
- **No Glossary / no Assumptions Index**: unconfirmed items are flagged in prose ("unconfirmed," "see §9") rather than with a literal `[ASSUMPTION: …]` convention collected in an index. Functionally similar, but an automated extraction pass looking for bracketed assumption tags would find none — worth knowing if downstream tooling expects that convention.
- **ID scheme asymmetry**: Student and Admin citations use ID prefixes (ST-xx, OP-xx); Driver citations use section numbers (§x.x) throughout §6. Internally consistent per-doc, but inconsistent across the table.
- **Shared citation, not a duplicate error**: Admin OP-25 is cited twice (M01 self-service account deletion, M15 admin review of account deletion) — this looks like a duplicate but is plausibly the same screen's two workflows; worth a one-line note if that ambiguity matters downstream.
- **Unresolved citation**: the M11 Admin "SOS alerts" cell (§6) is the one scope-table entry with no resolvable ID — already flagged under Done-ness clarity and Downstream usability above.
- **Verification limit on this review**: the three cited source docs (`user-stories-student.md`, `user-stories-driver.md`, `user-stories-admin.md`) were not present anywhere in this workspace at review time, so none of the cited IDs (ST-xx, Driver §x.x, OP-xx) could be checked against the actual documents — this review assessed the PRD's internal citation *structure* (completeness, consistency) but could not confirm the citations themselves resolve. The PM should confirm resolution directly before treating this PRD as a reliable index into those docs.
